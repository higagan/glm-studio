## Slide 3: title
# Era 1 — The Doordarshan Years
## 1959–1990s · One nation, one channel

## Slide 4: split
## Birth of Doordarshan
Left: **1959** — Experimental telecast from Delhi. **1976** — Doordarshan separates from All India Radio. **1982** — Asian Games spark colour TV & national expansion. One channel, evening-only slots, communal viewing.

Right: Shows became shared national rituals — families and neighbourhoods gathered around a single screen. Television was an event, not a background.

## Slide 5: bullets
## Doordarshan Milestones
- **1976** — Doordarshan established
- **1982** — Colour broadcast; Asian Games
- **1984–86** — *Hum Log*, *Buniyaad* (first soaps)
- **1986** — *Mahabharat* — Sunday-morning phenomenon
- **1989** — *Ramayan* — streets would empty during airtime

## Slide 7: title
# Era 2 — Cable & Satellite
## 1990s–2000s · The channel explosion

## Slide 8: split
## Doordarshan vs The Cable Era
Left: **Doordarshan era**
- Ownership: state monopoly
- Channels: 1–2
- Scheduling: fixed evening slots
- Genres: myth, soap, social
- Audience: shared national viewing

Right: **Cable & satellite era**
- Ownership: private, multi-broadcaster
- Channels: hundreds, multilingual
- Scheduling: 24×7 programming
- Genres: soaps, reality, news, music, kids
- Audience: fragmented & segmented

## Slide 9: bullets
## Soaps, Stars & Sunday Blockbusters
- *Kyunki Saas Bhi Kabhi Bahu Thi* (2000) — era-defining daily soap
- *Kaun Banega Crorepati* (2000) — reality/gameshow goes primetime
- Indian Idol, MTV, Channel V — youth & music programming
- **TRP race begins:** ratings drive scheduling & budgets
- Star, Zee, Sony, Colors reshape the competitive landscape

> In the cable era, families planned their evenings around the 9 PM soap.

## Slide 11: title
# Era 3 — Digital & OTT
## 2010s–present · The unbundling of TV

## Slide 12: split
## The Streaming Surge — India goes on-demand
Left: **By the numbers**
- **500M+** internet users by mid-2020s
- Among the **cheapest mobile data** globally
- Dozens of OTT platforms competing
- Originals & regional content expanding sharply

Right: **Major platforms**
- JioHotstar / Disney+ Hotstar
- Netflix · Amazon Prime Video
- ZEE5 · SonyLIV
- MX Player · JioCinema
- Linear TV still large, but growth is digital

## Slide 13: bullets
## Linear TV vs OTT — Two ways to watch
- **Access:** scheduled broadcast → on-demand streaming
- **Device:** living-room TV → phone, tablet, laptop, TV
- **Model:** channel bundle → subscription + freemium + AVOD
- **Content:** censored, family-safe → originals, mature, regional, global
- **Control:** broadcaster decides → viewer decides (pause, binge, skip)

## Slide 14: bullets
## The Full Arc, 1959 → 2025
- **1959** — First experimental telecast
- **1982** — Colour TV & Asian Games
- **1992–95** — Cable & satellite channels launch (Zee, Star, Sony)
- **2000** — Daily soaps + KBC redefine primetime
- **2015–20** — Mobile data + OTT platforms explode
- **2020s** — Streaming originals, regional wave, live sports on OTT

## Slide 15: split
## Conclusion — What the evolution tells us
Left: **What changed**
- Scarcity → abundance (one channel to infinite choice)
- Appointment viewing → on-demand control
- National consensus → fragmented, personal taste
- State gatekeeper → creator- & platform-led economy

Right: **What stayed constant**
- Storytelling remains central
- Audiences love serialized drama
- TV adapts to the screen people own
- The medium follows technology
